Short answer: **criminals earn more**. That’s on purpose. The developers built ROB RUN on high risk, high reward: criminals gamble their own gear every run, while cops get free equipment and earn less in return.

But “earns more” isn’t the whole story. What matters is how much you **keep**.

## Side by side

|                       | Cop                                          | Criminal                                  |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Earning potential     | Lower, by design                             | Higher                                    |
| Your gear at risk     | None. Only department gear                   | Your bag on death; equipped gear unless you surrender |
| Start-up cost         | Free gear and a patrol car                   | Tools, weapons, a car                     |
| How you get paid      | Arrests, confiscations, bribes               | Selling loot to the Fence                 |
| Availability          | Only when a police slot is open              | Any time                                  |
| Best for              | Rebuilding, learning to fight                | Big payouts when you’re geared up         |

## How cops get paid

Cops don’t get a fixed hourly wage. They earn from catching criminals:

- **Arrests:** you get a percentage of the value of the loot you confiscate. The richer the robber, the bigger your cut.
- **Arrest instead of kill:** an arrest pays more than a kill. Turning on the siren and ordering the criminal to surrender before arresting them gives extra.
- **Bribes:** a criminal with money in the bank can offer you a bribe. Accept it and they keep their bag, but you get paid.
- **Backup calls:** when the police are short on players, answering a backup call can give better gear and a temporary pay bonus.

Cop pay is cash, so it’s wiped at the weekly Federal Raid like everything else. Launder it.

## How criminals get paid

Criminals earn by **selling stolen loot to the Fence**. The more **Notoriety** you build during a masked spree, the more the Fence pays.

The upside has no real ceiling: bigger targets, better tools and a crew mean bigger hauls. The downside:

- Die in the city and you **lose your bag**.
- Get caught and you can **surrender** (lose the bag, keep what you’re wearing) or **bribe** the cop (keep everything, if they accept).
- Die in the desert and you **lose all your loot**.

## When playing cop is the smarter choice

- **You just lost everything.** A cop shift costs nothing and gets you cash for new tools.
- **You’re new to fighting.** Practising with free department gear is much cheaper than practising with your own.
- **The city is full of strong crews.** As a solo criminal, you’re their target. As a cop, they’re yours, and dying doesn’t cost you your gear.

## When playing criminal is the smarter choice

- **You’re geared up** and have a reliable route.
- **You’re in a crew** and can take bigger targets.
- **You have money in the bank** for bribes, so getting caught isn’t a total loss.
- **Police slots are full**, which happens a lot.

## The catch with playing cop: slots

Police slots are limited per city, and they fill up fast. If you want to play cop, you may need to wait for a slot or try a fresher server.

## The best of both: mix them

Most players don’t pick one forever. A good rhythm:

1. **Criminal runs** while you have gear and a plan.
2. Lose your gear? **Do a cop shift** to rebuild.
3. **Launder everything** you earn either way.

That way a bad run sets you back one shift, not one wipe.

## Verdict

**For the most money:** criminal.
**For the most reliable money:** cop.
**For new players:** start as a civilian, try a cop shift to learn the city and its fights, then move to small criminal runs.
